## 1. Caravaggio (Michelangelo Merisi da Caravaggio)

**Who:** Italian painter, born 29 September 1571 in Milan, died 18 July 1610 at Porto Ercole (Tuscany), aged 38.

**Story.** Caravaggio became the most talked-about painter in Rome around 1600 by painting saints with dirty feet and models taken off the street. His police record in Rome runs to sword-carrying charges, assaults, throwing a plate of artichokes at a waiter, and a 1603 libel trial for insulting verses about a rival painter, Giovanni Baglione. On 28 or 29 May 1606 he killed Ranuccio Tomassoni in a sword fight in the Campo Marzio; he was sentenced in absentia to a *bando capitale*, which meant anyone could kill him and claim a reward. He fled to Naples, then Malta, where he was made a Knight of the Order of St John in 1608, then jailed and expelled from the Order after another violent incident. In 1609 he was attacked outside the Osteria del Cerriglio in Naples and his face was badly cut. In 1610 he sailed toward Rome expecting a papal pardon, carrying paintings meant as gifts, and died at Porto Ercole on the way. A team led by Silvano Vinceti claimed in 2010 to have found his bones there, and a 2018 study in *The Lancet Infectious Diseases* (Drancourt et al.) found *Staphylococcus aureus* DNA in a tooth from that skeleton and proposed sepsis from his wounds.

**Why it works (strange / controversial / mysterious).** A celebrity killer who painted his own face on a severed head and mailed it to the man who could pardon him. Nobody agrees on why he killed Tomassoni (a tennis bet, a courtesan named Fillide Melandroni, Tomassoni's wife, or an honour duel; some historians even argue he was aiming to castrate him) or how he died (fever, sepsis, lead poisoning, or a revenge killing by the Knights of Malta).
**Open question to end on:** Did he die of an infected wound, or did someone finally collect the bounty?

**Hook candidate (strangest true detail):** In *David with the Head of Goliath* (Borghese), the bleeding severed head is Caravaggio's own self-portrait, and the painting was sent to Cardinal Scipione Borghese, the papal nephew who controlled pardons.
**Second surprise:** *The Beheading of Saint John the Baptist* in Valletta is the only painting he ever signed, and he wrote the signature ("f. MichelAn") in the blood spilling from the saint's neck.

## 2. Vincent van Gogh (the ear and the gun)

**Who:** Dutch painter, born 30 March 1853 in Zundert (Netherlands), died 29 July 1890 in Auvers-sur-Oise (France), aged 37.

**Story.** Van Gogh painted for only about ten years and sold almost nothing in his lifetime. On 23 December 1888 in Arles, after a fight with Paul Gauguin, he cut off his ear with a razor, wrapped it in paper and handed it at a brothel door to a young woman; the press called her "Rachel". Researcher Bernadette Murphy showed in 2016 (book *Van Gogh's Ear: The True Story*) that she was Gabrielle Berlatier, a farm girl working as a maid who had been bitten by a rabid dog, and found a sketch by his doctor Félix Rey (in Irving Stone's papers at the Bancroft Library, Berkeley) indicating he cut off almost the whole ear, not just the lobe. On 27 July 1890 he came back to his inn in Auvers with a bullet in his abdomen and died two days later; no gun was found at the time. In 2011 biographers Steven Naifeh and Gregory White Smith argued he was shot, perhaps accidentally, by teenager René Secrétan, who used to bully him and owned a gun. A rusted Lefaucheux revolver found in a field around 1960 was sold at Drouot in Paris in June 2019 for about EUR 162,500 including fees, billed as "the most famous weapon in art history". In 2020 the exact hillside of his last painting, *Tree Roots*, was identified from an old postcard, about 150 m from his inn.

**Why it works.** Everyone thinks they know this story, and nearly every popular version (lobe only, ear given to a prostitute, *Wheatfield with Crows* as the suicide note) is wrong or disputed.
**Open question:** Suicide, accident, or a teenager's prank gone wrong, and why would a dying man say he did it himself?

**Hook candidate:** He didn't cut off a bit of his ear. He cut off nearly all of it, and gave it to a farm girl who was being treated for a rabid dog bite.
**Second surprise:** His real last painting is not *Wheatfield with Crows* but the unfinished *Tree Roots*, and the exact roadside where he painted it was only located in 2020.

## 3. Francisco de Goya: the Black Paintings and the missing head

**Who:** Spanish painter, born 30 March 1746 in Fuendetodos (Aragon), died 16 April 1828 in Bordeaux (France), aged 82.

**Story.** Goya was First Court Painter to the Spanish king, and went completely deaf after an illness in 1792-93. Around 1819-1823, in his seventies, he painted fourteen dark murals in oil directly on the plaster walls of his country house outside Madrid, the Quinta del Sordo ("House of the Deaf Man"), and never titled, signed or mentioned them. They include Saturn devouring his son, two men beating each other with clubs, and a dog's head nearly swallowed by an empty slope. After his death the house changed hands; in 1874-78 the murals were peeled off the walls and transferred to canvas for the banker Baron Émile d'Erlanger, shown in Paris in 1878, and given to the Prado in 1881. In 2003 historian Juan José Junquera argued that the upper floor where half the murals were did not exist in Goya's time, so the paintings might be by someone else (possibly his son Javier); the Prado and Nigel Glendinning (2004) rejected this. Goya died in exile in Bordeaux; when Spanish officials exhumed him decades later for reburial in Madrid, the skeleton had no skull, and it has never been found. He now lies under his own frescoes in San Antonio de la Florida, Madrid, still without a head.

**Why it works.** Untitled nightmare paintings on a deaf old man's dining-room walls, a living attribution dispute, and a stolen skull (most historians think taken for phrenology).
**Open question:** Who took Goya's head, and is it sitting in someone's cabinet today?

**Hook candidate:** Spain's greatest painter is buried without his head. The consul reported a headless skeleton; Madrid's reply, as the story goes, was to send Goya "with or without head".
**Second surprise:** The Black Paintings were never meant for a museum; they were dining-room and sitting-room walls, photographed in place by J. Laurent in 1874 before being cut off.

## 4. Richard Dadd

**Who:** English painter, born 1 August 1817 in Chatham (Kent), died 8 January 1886 at Broadmoor Asylum (Berkshire), aged 68.

**Story.** Dadd was one of the most promising young painters of early Victorian London, known for fairy scenes from Shakespeare. In 1842-43 he travelled for about ten months through Europe and the Middle East as draughtsman for the lawyer Sir Thomas Phillips, and came back convinced he was being persecuted by devils and receiving messages from the Egyptian god Osiris. On 28 August 1843, in Cobham Park near Rochester, he stabbed his father to death, believing him to be the devil in disguise. He fled to France intending to kill more people, and was arrested after attacking a stranger with a razor in a coach. Found insane, he was never tried; he spent the remaining 42 years of his life in the criminal wing of Bethlem Hospital (from 22 August 1844) and, from 1864, at the new Broadmoor. Inside, encouraged by progressive doctors, he kept painting; *The Fairy Feller's Master-Stroke* took him nine years (1855-64) for the Bethlem steward George Henry Haydon, and was left unfinished when he was moved. In 1865 he wrote a long poem explaining the painting, mostly to say it means nothing in particular. Siegfried Sassoon gave the painting to the Tate in 1963 in memory of a friend who was Dadd's great-nephew, and Freddie Mercury wrote the Queen song "The Fairy Feller's Master-Stroke" (*Queen II*, 1974) after staring at it in the Tate.

**Why it works.** A father-killer who painted microscopic fairies for forty years in a madhouse, and a painting that a rock star turned into a song.
**Open question:** Is the Fairy Feller a coded picture of the murder (the raised axe, the father-figure watching) or, as Dadd insisted, about nothing at all?

**Hook candidate:** He killed his father because Osiris told him the man was the devil, then spent nine years in an asylum painting one tiny fairy about to crack one hazelnut.
**Second surprise:** The painting hangs in the Tate because the war poet Siegfried Sassoon donated it, and it inspired a Queen song.

## 5. Edvard Munch (The Scream's hidden sentence)

**Who:** Norwegian painter, born 12 December 1863 in Ådalsbruk (Løten), died 23 January 1944 at Ekely, Oslo, aged 80.

**Story.** Munch lost his mother and his favourite sister to tuberculosis as a child and said that "illness, insanity and death" stood by his cradle. In 1893 he made the first painted *Scream* (tempera and crayon on cardboard), and went on to make four colour versions plus a lithograph. In the upper-left corner of the 1893 painting, almost invisible in the red sky, someone wrote in pencil: "Kan kun være malet af en gal Mand!" ("Can only have been painted by a madman!"). For over a century people assumed a vandal had written it; in February 2021 Norway's National Museum used infrared imaging and handwriting comparison to show that Munch wrote it himself, probably after a 1895 student debate where a young doctor suggested his work proved he was mentally ill. The same painting carries white spots long blamed on bird droppings; in 2016 X-ray analysis at DESY (Hamburg) showed they are candle wax from his studio. *The Scream* was stolen twice: the National Gallery version on the opening day of the 1994 Lillehammer Olympics (thieves left a note thanking the museum for poor security), and the Munch Museum version, with *Madonna*, by armed men in 2004; both were recovered. In 1902 a quarrel with his fiancée Tulla Larsen ended with a revolver going off and destroying part of a finger on his left hand. The 1895 pastel version sold at Sotheby's in 2012 for USD 119.9 million.

**Why it works.** The most famous image of anxiety carries a hidden self-insult in the artist's own handwriting, plus thefts, a gunshot, and "bird poop" that wasn't.
**Open question:** Was "only a madman could have painted this" a joke at his critics' expense, or a confession?

**Hook candidate:** There is a tiny pencil sentence hidden in the sky of *The Scream*: "Can only have been painted by a madman!" For 117 years everyone blamed a vandal. It was Munch.
**Second surprise:** The figure in *The Scream* is not screaming; in Munch's own account he hears "an infinite scream passing through nature" and covers his ears.

## 6. Pieter Bruegel the Elder

**Who:** Netherlandish painter and print designer, born c. 1525-30 (place uncertain, probably near Breda), died 9 September 1569 in Brussels, aged about 40-45.

**Story.** Almost nothing about Bruegel's life is documented: no letters, no diary, no certain birthplace, and only about 40 surviving paintings. Most of what we "know" comes from Karel van Mander's *Schilder-boeck* (1604), written 35 years after his death. Van Mander says Bruegel and a merchant friend, Hans Franckert, dressed up as peasants to crash country weddings and fairs, bringing gifts as if they were relatives, so he could study how people ate, danced and fought; people nicknamed him "Pier den Drol" (Peter the Joker). He painted crowds packed with dozens of tiny stories: over a hundred proverbs acted out in one village (*Netherlandish Proverbs*), an army of skeletons wiping out humanity (*Triumph of Death*), a tower so big it has its own weather (*Tower of Babel*). He worked in the years when Spanish troops were crushing religious dissent in the Low Countries, and his *Massacre of the Innocents* shows soldiers in contemporary uniforms killing babies in a Flemish village. In that painting, now in the British Royal Collection, a later owner had the murdered babies painted over with bundles, food and animals; it was in Emperor Rudolf II's collection in Prague by about 1600, and the overpainting was identified during conservation in 1998. Van Mander also reports that on his deathbed Bruegel had his wife burn a set of drawings whose inscriptions were too biting, out of remorse or fear that they would get her into trouble.

**Why it works.** A painter with no biography who left behind crowded "Where's Wally?" puzzles, a censored massacre, and a bonfire of secret drawings.
**Open question:** What was in the drawings he had burned, and who was he afraid of?

**Hook candidate:** Someone took Bruegel's painting of soldiers murdering babies and painted the babies over with sausages, cheeses and parcels. You can still see it at the British Royal Collection.
**Second surprise (gossip gold for CCC):** According to van Mander he left *The Magpie on the Gallows* to his wife, explaining that the magpie stood for gossips, "whom he would deliver to the gallows".

## 7. Egon Schiele

**Who:** Austrian painter, born 12 June 1890 in Tulln (Lower Austria), died 31 October 1918 in Vienna, aged 28.

**Story.** A protégé of Gustav Klimt, Schiele made raw, twisted, often explicit drawings of himself and his models, and left Vienna with his partner and model Wally Neuzil to live in small towns. In April 1912 in Neulengbach he was arrested after a 13-year-old girl who had run away from home stayed with the couple; the charges of seduction and abduction were dropped, but he was convicted of exhibiting "immoral" drawings in a place where children could see them. More than a hundred drawings were seized, and at the hearing the judge burned one of them over a candle flame. He spent 24 days in custody and made a series of watercolours of his cell, inscribed with lines like "The one orange was the only light". In 1915 he left Wally to marry a "respectable" neighbour, Edith Harms; Wally died of scarlet fever as a war nurse in 1917. Schiele died of the Spanish flu in October 1918, three days after his pregnant wife. His afterlife is a century of legal fights: his *Portrait of Wally* was seized in New York in 1998 as Nazi loot and settled in 2010 for USD 19 million, and since 2023 the Manhattan District Attorney has seized Schiele drawings from US museums (including the Art Institute of Chicago's *Russian War Prisoner*) as works looted from the Jewish cabaret star Fritz Grünbaum, who died in Dachau in 1941.

**Why it works.** A jailed 22-year-old with a judge burning his art in court, a model he dumped who died in the war, and paintings still being seized by prosecutors today.
**Open question:** Who really owns a Schiele, and was the 1912 case persecution of an artist or protection of a child?

**Hook candidate:** In 1912 a judge held one of Schiele's drawings over a candle and burned it in the courtroom.
**Second surprise:** Over a century later, his drawings are still being "arrested": the Manhattan DA seized works from US museums in 2023 as Nazi loot.

## 8. Han van Meegeren (the forger who sold a fake Vermeer to Göring)

**Who:** Dutch painter and forger, born 10 October 1889 in Deventer, died 30 December 1947 in Amsterdam, aged 58.

**Story.** Van Meegeren was a conservative portrait painter whom critics dismissed as old-fashioned; he decided to prove them wrong by forging the most admired Dutch master, Johannes Vermeer. He bought genuine 17th-century canvases, mixed his paints with Bakelite resin and baked the paintings in an oven so they would harden and crack like 300-year-old paint. In 1937 his *Supper at Emmaus* was hailed by the leading Vermeer expert, Abraham Bredius, as a masterpiece and bought for the Museum Boijmans in Rotterdam. During the German occupation he sold more fake Vermeers for fortunes; one, *Christ and the Adulteress*, went to Hermann Göring in exchange for 137 looted paintings. In May 1945 the Allies found it in Göring's hoard, traced the sale, and arrested van Meegeren for selling a Dutch national treasure to the enemy, a crime of collaboration that could carry the death penalty. To save himself he confessed that it was a fake he had painted, and when nobody believed him he painted a new "Vermeer", *Jesus among the Doctors*, under supervision in front of witnesses. In November 1947 he was cleared of treason, convicted of forgery and fraud, and sentenced to one year; he died of a heart attack before serving it, by then a popular hero for having "fooled Göring".

**Why it works.** The only man who saved his own life by proving he was a fraud, and a nation that turned a con man into a hero.
**Open question:** Hero who swindled the Nazis, or a Nazi-friendly crook who got rich in the war and rewrote his story?

**Hook candidate:** Accused of treason, he had one defence: "I didn't sell Göring a Vermeer. I painted it." Then he painted another one in front of the court's witnesses.
**Second surprise:** The fakes look obviously un-Vermeer today (the faces resemble 1930s film stars), yet the top expert of his time called the *Emmaus* "the masterpiece of Johannes Vermeer".

## 9. Artemisia Gentileschi

**Who:** Italian Baroque painter, born 8 July 1593 in Rome, died after 1654 in Naples (possibly in the 1656 plague; exact date unknown).

**Story.** Artemisia trained in the studio of her father, the painter Orazio Gentileschi, and was painting at a professional level by 17. In 1611 she was raped by Agostino Tassi, a painter working with her father, who then promised to marry her; when he didn't, Orazio took him to court in 1612. During the seven-month trial, Artemisia was interrogated under torture with the *sibille*, cords tightened around her fingers, to test whether she was telling the truth; the transcript records her repeating "È vero, è vero, è vero" ("It's true, it's true, it's true"). Tassi was convicted and sentenced to exile from Rome, a sentence that was never enforced. Days after the trial she was married off and moved to Florence, where she became the first woman admitted to the Accademia delle Arti del Disegno (1616) and worked for the Medici. She painted women from the Bible and myth who fight back, above all Judith beheading Holofernes, a version far more violent than Caravaggio's. She ran her own workshop in Naples, wrote sharp letters to collectors about being underpaid as a woman, and then nearly disappeared from art history until feminist scholars revived her in the 1970s.

**Why it works.** A rape trial in which the victim was the one tortured, a revenge-looking masterpiece, and a death date nobody knows.
**Open question:** Is her Judith a self-portrait of revenge on Tassi, or have we been reading her rape into every painting she made?

**Hook candidate:** At her rapist's trial, she was the one tortured: cords were tightened around a painter's fingers to see if she was lying.
**Second surprise:** Tassi was found guilty and banished from Rome, and never left.

## 10. Franz Xaver Messerschmidt (the grimacing heads)

**Who:** German-Austrian sculptor, born 6 February 1736 in Wiesensteig (Swabia), died 19 August 1783 in Pressburg (today Bratislava), aged 47.

**Story.** Messerschmidt was a successful court sculptor in Vienna, making official portraits of Empress Maria Theresa and her family, and taught at the Vienna Academy while waiting for a full professorship. In the 1770s he was passed over (contemporary documents speak of "confusion in his head" and odd behaviour), resigned, and eventually settled in Pressburg. There, from about 1770 until his death, he made a series of busts of a bald man, mostly himself, pulling extreme faces: yawning, sneering, pinching his lips, squeezing his eyes shut, with a strip of cloth over the mouth. He called them *Kopfstücke* ("head pieces") and reckoned they covered 64 expressions. The writer Friedrich Nicolai visited him in 1781 and reported that Messerschmidt believed a "Spirit of Proportion" tormented him at night, jealous because he had discovered the secret of proportion, and that he made each face by pinching himself under the ribs and grimacing at a mirror. After his death 49 heads were exhibited in Vienna in 1793 under made-up titles like *The Vexed Man*, *A Hypocrite and a Slanderer* and *Just Rescued from Drowning*, then shown as curiosities in the Prater amusement park. In 1932 the art historian and psychoanalyst Ernst Kris diagnosed him with paranoid psychosis from the heads, and that label stuck.

**Why it works.** Sixty-odd faces of one man fighting an invisible spirit, and they look exactly like modern meme reaction faces.
**Open question:** Was he a madman defending himself against demons, or an Enlightenment scientist running the first systematic study of facial expression?

**Hook candidate:** An 18th-century sculptor believed a demon called "the Spirit of Proportion" attacked him at night, so he pinched himself in front of a mirror and carved the faces he made: over sixty of them.
**Second surprise:** The titles everyone uses (*The Vexed Man*, *The Arch-Villain*) were not his; they were invented by an anonymous showman for a 1793 exhibition after his death.

## 11. Louis Wain (the "schizophrenic cats" that may be a myth)

**Who:** English illustrator, born 5 August 1860 in Clerkenwell, London, died 4 July 1939 at Napsbury Hospital (Hertfordshire), aged 78.

**Story.** Wain married his sisters' governess, Emily Richardson, ten years older than him, in 1884, a minor scandal at the time. Emily developed breast cancer, and Wain drew their black-and-white kitten Peter to amuse her; she died in 1887, and the cat drawings made him the most famous cat artist in Britain, with cats walking upright, playing golf and drinking tea in hundreds of books, annuals and postcards. He was hopeless with money, signed away his copyrights, and supported his mother and five unmarried sisters; one sister was certified insane. He held strange beliefs about electricity and cats and grew increasingly paranoid, and in 1924 he was certified insane and placed in the pauper wing of Springfield Hospital in Tooting. When his situation was discovered in 1925, a public appeal backed by H. G. Wells and Prime Minister Ramsay MacDonald moved him to Bethlem and then Napsbury, where he kept painting cats until his death. After he died, the psychiatrist Walter Maclay arranged eight cat paintings into a sequence, from normal cats to electric, fractal "kaleidoscope cats", and textbooks used it for decades as a picture of schizophrenia destroying the mind. The paintings were undated, Wain kept drawing ordinary cats to the end, and modern researchers (and the Bethlem Museum of the Mind) say there is no evidence for the order at all; his diagnosis itself is disputed (schizophrenia, dementia, autism have all been proposed).

**Why it works.** One of the most famous "images of madness" in psychology may be a curated fiction, and it is about cats.
**Open question:** Did his mind really dissolve into patterns, or did a psychiatrist shuffle a few paintings into the story he wanted to tell?

**Hook candidate:** That viral chart of cat paintings "showing a schizophrenic artist losing his mind"? Nobody knows what order they were painted in.
**Second surprise:** The Prime Minister of Britain and H. G. Wells campaigned to get a cat illustrator out of a pauper's asylum.

## 12. Arnold Böcklin (Isle of the Dead)

**Who:** Swiss Symbolist painter, born 16 October 1827 in Basel, died 16 January 1901 in San Domenico di Fiesole, near Florence, aged 73.

**Story.** In 1880, in his Florence studio, Böcklin was painting a rocky island with cypresses when a young widow, Marie Berna, asked him for "a picture to dream by"; he added a small boat in which a white-shrouded figure stands before a coffin, rowed toward the island. He painted five versions between 1880 and 1886 (Basel, New York, Berlin, a fourth destroyed in the Second World War, and Leipzig); his dealer Fritz Gurlitt gave it the title *Die Toteninsel*, "Isle of the Dead". The image became an obsession in central Europe: reproductions hung in countless homes, Rachmaninoff wrote his symphonic poem *Isle of the Dead* (1909) from a black-and-white print, and Freud, Lenin and Clemenceau were all said to own copies. Adolf Hitler, a passionate Böcklin admirer, bought the third version (1883) in 1933 and hung it at the Berghof and later in the Reich Chancellery; it is now in the Alte Nationalgalerie, Berlin. In the third version a burial chamber cut into the rock on the right carries the initials "A.B.", Böcklin's own. Nobody has ever established who the white figure is, who is in the coffin, or whether the island is a real place (candidates include the English Cemetery in Florence, near his studio, and Pontikonisi off Corfu).

**Why it works.** The most-reproduced "mood" painting of its time, a painter who reserved his own tomb in it, and the dictator who owned it.
**Open question:** Who is the figure in white, and does Hitler's ownership change how we see it?

**Hook candidate:** Böcklin painted his own initials on one of the tombs on the Isle of the Dead, and the dictator who later owned it hung it in his office.
**Second surprise:** Rachmaninoff said he might not have composed his *Isle of the Dead* if he had seen the original in colour first.

## 13. Adolf Wölfli

**Who:** Swiss artist, writer and composer, born 29 February 1864 in Bowil (canton of Bern), died 6 November 1930 at the Waldau psychiatric clinic near Bern, aged 66.

**Story.** Wölfli grew up in extreme poverty; after his father left, he was separated from his mother at about eight and sent to work as a *Verdingkind*, a farm child-labourer, where he was abused. As an adult labourer he was convicted in 1890 of attempting to sexually abuse young girls and served two years; arrested again in 1895 for a similar attempt on a small child, he was declared insane and committed to the Waldau asylum, where he stayed for the remaining 35 years of his life. From 1899 he began to draw, and over the next three decades he produced an autobiographical epic of about 25,000 pages: 45 large volumes and 16 school notebooks of text, drawings, collages, invented maps and musical scores. In it he rewrote his miserable childhood as a fantastical world tour, crowned himself "St. Adolf II", built a "Giant Creation" that would make him lord of the universe, and invented numbers far larger than any that exist. The young psychiatrist Walter Morgenthaler published *Ein Geisteskranker als Künstler* (A Psychiatric Patient as Artist) about him in 1921, one of the first books to treat a patient as an artist; Jean Dubuffet later made him a founding figure of Art Brut. His estate is held by the Adolf Wölfli Foundation at the Kunstmuseum Bern.

**Why it works.** A sex offender locked up for life who built a 25,000-page universe with a pencil ration, and an art world that reveres him.
**Open question:** Can we celebrate the art of a man locked up for abusing children, and was he ever "the artist" or just a patient on display?

**Hook candidate:** Every Monday he got one new pencil. It was gone in two days. With that, he made 25,000 pages.
**Second surprise:** He wrote music for his own works and played it on a trumpet rolled out of paper.

## 14. Remedios Varo (STRONG CONTENT, BLOCKED BY COPYRIGHT)

**Who:** Spanish-born Mexican Surrealist painter, born María de los Remedios Alicia Rodriga Varo y Uranga on 16 December 1908 in Anglès (Girona, Catalonia), died 8 October 1963 in Mexico City, aged 54.

**Story.** Her father, a hydraulic engineer, taught her technical drawing, which shows in the machines, gears and architecture of her paintings. She studied at the Real Academia de San Fernando in Madrid, joined the Surrealists in Barcelona and then Paris with the poet Benjamin Péret, and fled occupied France in 1941 via Marseille to Mexico City, where she lived among exiles including her close friend Leonora Carrington. For years she earned money with commercial work (advertising for the pharmaceutical company Bayer) and in the late 1940s spent time in Venezuela as a scientific illustrator, drawing insects for a public-health campaign. She only painted full-time from the mid-1950s; her first solo show (1956) sold out, and in barely seven years she produced the dreamlike, alchemical paintings that made her famous, with long-faced figures who sew the world, weave light or travel up rivers in contraptions. She also loved hoaxes: in 1959 she built *Homo rodans*, a "fossil" of a wheeled human ancestor assembled from chicken, turkey and fish bones left over from cooking, with a fake scientific treatise in mock-Latin signed by an invented German anthropologist ("Hälikcio von Fuhrängschmidt"). She died suddenly of a heart attack at 54. Her estate became a legal battle: her widower Walter Gruen gave and lent works to Mexico's Museo de Arte Moderno, there were court cases over ownership and heirship, and Mexico declared her work national artistic heritage, restricting export. Her market has exploded: *Armonía* sold for USD 6.2 million at Sotheby's in 2020 and *Revelación o El relojero* for USD 6.22 million at Christie's in May 2025.

**Why it works.** Witchy, scientific, funny: a surrealist who faked a fossil and treated paintings like alchemical experiments, plus an inheritance fight and record prices.
**Open question:** Was she painting magic or science, and whose is she: Spain's or Mexico's?

**Hook candidate:** She built a fake human ancestor out of chicken bones from her kitchen and wrote a fake scientific paper to prove it.
**Second surprise:** Thomas Pynchon built a key scene of *The Crying of Lot 49* (1966) around her painting *Embroidering the Earth's Mantle*.
