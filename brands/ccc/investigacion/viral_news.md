## Comedian - the $120,000 / $6.2M duct-taped banana (Maurizio Cattelan)

**Dates and places:** Art Basel Miami Beach (Perrotin booth), 4-8 Dec 2019; Leeum Museum of Art, Seoul, 27 Apr 2023; Sotheby's New York, 20 Nov 2024; The Peninsula hotel, Hong Kong, 29 Nov 2024; Centre Pompidou-Metz, 12 Jul 2025 and 30 May 2026.

### 1. The story
- On 4-6 Dec 2019 Perrotin sold editions of Cattelan's *Comedian* - a fresh banana duct-taped to a wall - for US$120,000 each (edition of three plus two artist's proofs); Cattelan reportedly bought the bananas at a Miami grocery for about 30 cents.
- On Sat 7 Dec 2019 performance artist David Datuna peeled the banana off the wall and ate it in front of the crowd, calling his act "Hungry Artist"; the gallery replaced the banana within the hour ("The banana is the idea").
- On Sun 8 Dec 2019 Perrotin removed the work at 9 a.m. because queues of selfie-takers created "a serious health and safety risk"; the *New York Post* put the banana on its cover and brands (Popeyes, Burger King, the NY Mets, Absolut, Perrier...) taped their products to walls.
- In Sep 2020 one edition was donated to the Guggenheim; in Jun 2023 a US federal judge dismissed a copyright suit by artist Joe Morford, who claimed he taped a banana to a wall first (2000).
- On 27 Apr 2023 a Seoul National University art student ate the banana at the Leeum in Seoul and taped the peel back up, telling staff he had skipped breakfast; Cattelan said "No problem at all."
- On 20 Nov 2024 Sotheby's sold an edition for US$5.2M hammer / US$6.24M with fees (estimate $1-1.5M) after about seven bidders; buyer: Tron crypto founder Justin Sun. The banana itself had been bought that morning from a fruit stand on New York's Upper East Side for 25 cents.
- On 29 Nov 2024 Sun ate it at a press conference in Hong Kong and pledged to buy 100,000 bananas from the 74-year-old stand worker, Shah Alam, who earns $12/hour.
- The banana keeps disappearing: a visitor ate it at Pompidou-Metz on 12 Jul 2025, and on 30 May 2026 visitors stole the banana and the tape there; the museum filed a complaint against persons unknown.

### 2. Why it went viral
Mechanic: **absurd price for a nothing object + the "anyone can eat it" stunt that keeps repeating**. Every new chapter (sale, eating, auction, eating again, theft) restarts the cycle; it is a meme-ready image a child can understand.
- Art Basel had to physically remove the work because crowds were unmanageable (InsideHook, NYT, Bloomberg, 8 Dec 2019).
- Brand-meme wave: Popeyes, Burger King, NY Mets, Hostess, Perrier, Carrefour, Sweetgreen, Absolut, Bobbi Brown copied it within 4 days (Ad Age, 10 Dec 2019); *New York Post* front page.
- 2024 eating: BBC News TikTok clip about 202,400 likes / 3,442 comments; Corriere della Sera TikTok about 149,700 likes (TikTok, accessed Nov 2026). Hundreds of syndicated copies of the AP/Reuters story (Fox affiliates, NPR stations, SCMP, Gulf News...).
- Google Trends: not measured directly (Trends could not be queried from this session); unverified.

### 3. Open question to end on
If you can eat the artwork, pay $6.2M for a certificate and the banana gets stolen twice a year... what exactly did Justin Sun buy? / Is the art the banana, the paper, or the people who keep eating it?

### 4. Strangest true detail
The banana that became a $6.2 million artwork was bought that morning for 25 cents from a man earning $12 an hour across the street - and he only learned the price from reporters.

## Soup on Van Gogh's Sunflowers (Just Stop Oil)

**Date and place:** 14 Oct 2022, Room 43, National Gallery, London. Follow-ups: sentencing 27 Sep 2024 (and a second "souping" the same day); appeals dismissed Mar 2025.

### 1. The story
- Shortly after 11 a.m. on 14 Oct 2022, Just Stop Oil activists Phoebe Plummer (21) and Anna Holland (20) opened two cans of Heinz tomato soup and threw them over Van Gogh's *Sunflowers* (1888).
- They then glued their hands to the wall under the painting; Plummer gave a speech asking "What is worth more, art or life?"
- The painting was behind glass: the National Gallery said there was minor damage to the frame and the painting was unharmed; it was back on the wall the same afternoon.
- The demand was that the UK government stop licensing new oil and gas projects; the stunt kicked off a global wave of "museum actions" (mashed potato on a Monet in Potsdam on 23 Oct 2022, Vermeer's *Girl with a Pearl Earring* in The Hague on 27 Oct 2022, and more).
- On 27 Sep 2024 Southwark Crown Court jailed Plummer for two years and Holland for 20 months for criminal damage; the judge said soup could have seeped behind the glass.
- Hours later, three other activists threw soup on two more Sunflowers paintings at the same gallery.
- In Mar 2025 the Court of Appeal ([2025] EWCA Crim 199) dismissed both women's sentence appeals; Just Stop Oil announced the end of its direct-action campaign in spring 2025.

### 2. Why it went viral
Mechanic: **destruction-on-camera + culture-war split** (climate kids vs. art lovers, "do you care more about a painting or the planet?"). The clip is 10 seconds of pure shock, with a scripted speech that people quoted, mocked and parodied for weeks; it became the template for a whole protest genre.
- Axios (26 Oct 2022): the footage had about **49.6 million views on Twitter alone**; Fortune: one of the activists' own videos passed 11 million views.
- Global coverage on day one: Al Jazeera, CBS, ABC/GMA, Irish Times, Global News, NL Times, Taipei Times, Artnet, etc.; copycat actions in Germany, the Netherlands, Italy, Australia, France.
- Conspiracy/meme layer: viral posts falsely claimed the activists were funded by an oil heiress (partly true: the Climate Emergency Fund, co-founded by Aileen Getty, funded JSO - a real "gossip" angle).

### 3. Open question to end on
Did the soup make anyone care more about the climate - or only about the frame? / Was it vandalism, or the most effective 10-second ad of the decade?

### 4. Strangest true detail
The painting never got wet - it was behind glass - yet two women went to prison for up to two years over the 17th-century-style frame, and while they were being sentenced, others threw soup at two more Sunflowers in the same building.

### 5. Footage
- Just Stop Oil's own video of the action (released by the group on X/Twitter, 14 Oct 2022; copyright of the group; widely used by news under fair dealing).
- Reuters / AFP / PA agency video of the room and of the activists glued to the wall (licensed).

## Salvator Mundi - the $450 million "last Leonardo" that vanished

**Date and place:** Sold 15 Nov 2017, Christie's, Rockefeller Center, New York. Not seen in public since. Location still unconfirmed (Nov 2026).

### 1. The story
- In 1958 the painting, heavily overpainted and thought to be a copy, sold at Sotheby's London for £45; in 2005 two dealers bought it at a small New Orleans-area auction for about US$1,175.
- After a long restoration by Dianne Modestini it was shown as a Leonardo at the National Gallery London (2011); dealer Yves Bouvier sold it in 2013 to Russian billionaire Dmitry Rybolovlev for US$127.5M, sparking a global fraud dispute between them.
- Christie's marketed it as "the last da Vinci" in an unusual campaign (a video filmed people staring at it, including Leonardo DiCaprio) and put it in a contemporary-art evening sale.
- On 15 Nov 2017, after about 19 minutes of phone bidding, it sold for US$450.3M with fees (US$400M hammer) - nearly 2.5x the previous auction record (Picasso, US$179.4M); the room applauded.
- The buyer was revealed as Saudi Prince Badr bin Abdullah, widely reported to be acting for Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman; it was announced for the Louvre Abu Dhabi.
- In Sep 2018 the Louvre Abu Dhabi unveiling was cancelled without explanation; it was absent from the Louvre's 2019 Leonardo blockbuster in Paris.
- Reports since: on MBS's yacht *Serene* (Artnet, 2019); in storage in Geneva (The Art Newspaper, Aug 2024, citing scholar Bernard Haykel); planned centrepiece of a future Saudi museum due around 2026. As of Nov 2026 no public showing has been confirmed.
- Documentaries *The Lost Leonardo* (2021) and *The Savior for Sale* (2021) aired claims that experts doubted Leonardo painted most of it.

### 2. Why it went viral
Mechanic: **absurd price + open mystery (is it real? where is it?) + royal/oligarch gossip**. It is the most expensive painting ever sold, it may not be by Leonardo, and nobody knows where it is.
- Instant meme cycle: Seth Rogen and critic Jerry Saltz tweets, "dashboard Jesus" memes, the "Jesus orb" jokes (W Magazine, 17 Nov 2017; Artnet "meme canon").
- Record coverage: PBS, Smithsonian, Fortune, NBC, BBC, every major paper; two feature documentaries; repeated "missing painting" waves in 2019, 2021, 2024.
- Multiple uploads of the hammer moment on YouTube (NBC News, Christie's, etc.) - view counts not retrievable from this session (unverified).

### 3. Open question to end on
Where is the world's most expensive painting right now - a yacht, a Geneva freeport, or a museum that never opens? And did anyone really pay $450 million for a Leonardo... or for a story?

### 4. Strangest true detail
It sold for £45 in 1958 and for US$450,312,500 in 2017 - and the glass orb in Christ's hand does not distort the background the way real glass would, one of the main arguments sceptics use against Leonardo's authorship.

- NBC News report "Da Vinci portrait of Christ sells for record $450.3 million": https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gmou0Da0OnM (NBC, copyrighted)
- The painting image: Leonardo's works are public domain; Commons has the post-restoration image: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Leonardo_da_Vinci,_Salvator_Mundi,_c.1500,_oil_on_walnut,_45.4_%C3%97_65.6_cm.jpg (verify file page before use; PD-Art).

## Beeple: the $69 million JPEG (2021) ... and the billionaire-head robot dogs (2025)

**Dates and places:** Online sale at Christie's, closed 11 Mar 2021; Art Basel Miami Beach (Zero 10 sector), 3-7 Dec 2025.

### 1. The story
- Mike Winkelmann ("Beeple"), a Wisconsin-born graphic designer, made one digital picture every day from 1 May 2007 to 7 Jan 2021; *Everydays: The First 5000 Days* is a single JPEG collage of all of them, sold as an NFT.
- Christie's opened online bidding at US$100 on 25 Feb 2021, the first time a major auction house sold a purely digital NFT and accepted ether.
- On 11 Mar 2021 it sold for US$69,346,250 with fees (US$60.25M hammer) - the third-highest auction price ever for a living artist, behind Jeff Koons and David Hockney; Christie's said 22 million visitors tuned in during the final minutes.
- The buyer was revealed on 12 Mar 2021 as "Metakovan" (Vignesh Sundaresan), Singapore-based founder of the NFT fund Metapurse, who later claimed the work was worth US$1 billion; Beeple himself said NFT prices were "absolutely" a bubble.
- The NFT market then collapsed through 2022-2023, and the sale became the symbol of the crypto-art bubble.
- On 3 Dec 2025, at the Art Basel Miami Beach VIP preview, Beeple unveiled *Regular Animals*: robot dogs wearing hyper-realistic heads of Elon Musk, Mark Zuckerberg, Jeff Bezos, Andy Warhol, Pablo Picasso and Beeple himself.
- The dogs photograph visitors, re-style the photos with AI and print them out of their rear end ("poop mode"); each edition sold for US$100,000 and all sold out, Art Basel told Fox Business.

### 2. Why it went viral
Mechanic: **absurd price for "nothing you can touch" (2021) + uncanny billionaire satire (2025)**. The 2021 sale made "NFT" a household word overnight; the 2025 robot dogs were pure meme fuel (Musk's face on a dog pooping pictures).
- Christie's: 22 million visitors during the final minutes of the 2021 sale (Christie's, via The Hill / Art Wolf).
- 2021 coverage: Bloomberg, Al Jazeera, CoinDesk, Artnet, BBC, CNN; "NFT" Google searches spiked in March 2021 (widely reported; Trends not queried here - unverified).
- 2025: "video goes viral" headlines in Business Today, Fox Business, PopSci, Scientific American, Designboom, Slashgear ("might be the worst thing you see all week") within 48 hours.

### 3. Open question to end on
Who is the real artwork - the dog, the billionaire on top, or the people paying $100,000 to watch it poop? / Was the $69 million JPEG the start of a new art world, or the top of a bubble?

### 4. Strangest true detail
The robot dog with Elon Musk's face takes your photo, "reinterprets" it with AI and prints it out of its backside - and every edition sold.

## "America" - Cattelan's solid-gold toilet stolen from Blenheim Palace

**Dates and places:** Guggenheim New York, Sep 2016-2017; Blenheim Palace, Oxfordshire (Churchill's birthplace), stolen 14 Sep 2019; Oxford Crown Court trial Feb-Mar 2025, sentences May-Jun 2025; second toilet sold at Sotheby's New York, 18 Nov 2025.

### 1. The story
- Maurizio Cattelan made *America*, a fully working toilet in 18-carat gold (about 98 kg), and installed it in a Guggenheim restroom in 2016; over 100,000 people queued to use it.

- In Sep 2019 it was plumbed into a bathroom at Blenheim Palace for an exhibition; visitors could book 3-minute slots.
- Two days after the opening, before 5 a.m. on 14 Sep 2019, a gang smashed in with sledgehammers, ripped it out - flooding the listed palace - and drove off in under five minutes; it was insured for £4.75M.
- The gold has never been recovered and is believed to have been cut up and melted down.
- In 2025 James Sheen (planner, already serving 19+ years) pleaded guilty and got 4 years; Michael Jones (reconnaissance visits) was convicted and got 27 months; Fred Doe, who helped sell the gold, got a suspended sentence.
- On 18 Nov 2025 the only other edition sold at Sotheby's New York for US$12.1M with fees after a single bid at about the value of its gold; the buyer was Ripley's Believe It or Not!.

### 2. Why it went viral
Mechanic: **crime + toilet humour + absurd luxury + political jab (Trump)**. "Thieves steal a working gold toilet from Churchill's birthplace" is a headline that writes its own jokes.

## An AI picture wins the Colorado State Fair art contest

**Date and place:** Prize announced late Aug 2022 (Allen posted on 25 Aug; viral from 30 Aug 2022), Colorado State Fair, Pueblo, Colorado. Copyright refusal Sep 2023; lawsuit filed 26 Sep 2024, pending.

### 1. The story
- Jason M. Allen, a tabletop-game designer from Pueblo West, made *Théâtre D'opéra Spatial* with the text-to-image tool Midjourney, then touched it up in Photoshop and upscaled it.
- He entered it, labelled "Jason M. Allen via Midjourney", in the fair's "Digital Arts / Digitally-Manipulated Photography" category, and it won first place and a US$300 prize.
- On 25 Aug 2022 he posted about the win on Midjourney's Discord; on 30 Aug a tweet by an artist calling it "pretty f***ing shitty" exploded and the story went global within 48 hours.
- The judges said they had not known Midjourney was an AI tool but would still have given it the prize; the fair said the entry was within the rules because AI use was disclosed.
- Allen told the New York Times (2 Sep 2022): "Art is dead, dude. It's over. A.I. won. Humans lost."
- On 5 Sep 2023 the US Copyright Office Review Board refused to register the image because the human contribution was too small (he said he used at least 624 prompts).
- On 26 Sep 2024 Allen sued the Register of Copyrights in federal court in Colorado (*Allen v. Perlmutter*); both sides filed for summary judgment in 2025 and no ruling had been reported as of Nov 2026 (unverified - check the docket).

### 2. Why it went viral
Mechanic: **culture-war fight (artists vs. AI) + "the machine won" moment**. It was the first mainstream proof that AI images could beat humans in a real contest, landing just as Midjourney/DALL-E/Stable Diffusion exploded.
- The viral tweet that broke it reportedly drew close to 90,000 likes and 13,000 retweets (Hyperallergic; figure may belong to a reply - treat as approximate).
- Coverage in NYT, CNN, Washington Post, PetaPixel, PC Gamer, Hyperallergic, Vice, BBC; listed in the AI Incident Database (incident report 2162).
- Allen received death threats and online harassment (CPR, IPWatchdog) - the fight kept going for three years via the copyright case.

### 3. Open question to end on
If a human typed 624 prompts, who is the artist - the man, the machine, or the millions of artists the machine learned from? And should the $300 be given back?

### 4. Strangest true detail
The prize that changed the art world's mood was worth only US$300 - and the judges said they would have picked it anyway.

### 5. Footage

## "Take the Money and Run" - artist gets paid in cash, delivers two blank canvases

**Date and place:** Delivered Sep 2021 for the show "Work It Out" (opened 24 Sep 2021), Kunsten Museum of Modern Art, Aalborg, Denmark; Copenhagen City Court ruling 18 Sep 2023; appeal filed.

### 1. The story
- Kunsten asked Danish conceptual artist Jens Haaning to recreate two earlier works that displayed real banknotes, showing average annual Danish and Austrian salaries, and lent him about 532,000 kroner (about US$76,000) in cash to put in the frames, plus an artist's fee.
- When the crates arrived in Sep 2021, the frames were empty; Haaning had titled the new work *Take the Money and Run*.
- He told Danish radio: "The work is that I have taken their money" - and said it was not theft but a breach of contract that was part of the work, a protest against poor pay for artists.
- The museum laughed, hung the empty frames anyway, and the show ran until Jan 2022 - while asking for the money back by the end of the exhibition.
- He did not return it; the museum sued.
- On 18 Sep 2023 the Copenhagen City Court ordered him to repay 492,549 kroner (about US$70,000) - the loan minus his 40,000-kroner fee - calling the delivery "deficient" and dismissing his copyright counter-claim; his lawyer appealed (outcome not found in English-language sources; check Danish press "Haaning landsret").

### 2. Why it went viral
Mechanic: **a prank + "anyone could have done that" + Robin-Hood class anger (artists are underpaid)**. The title is the punchline; the story has two viral moments (2021 prank, 2023 court ruling).
- 2021: AP, BBC, CNN, Guardian, NPR, plus meme pages; 2023 ruling: AP via dozens of US stations (Fox affiliates, GPB, KEYT/CNN Style), Hypebeast, Boing Boing, NZ Herald.
- Exact view counts unverified.

### 3. Open question to end on
Is it fraud - or the most honest artwork about money ever made? And if the empty frames were hung and visited... did the museum get what it paid for?

### 4. Strangest true detail
The museum actually exhibited the empty frames for four months - the "stolen" money became the show.

## Salvatore Garau sells an invisible sculpture for EUR 15,000

**Date and place:** Milan "Buddha in Contemplation" tape square, Piazza della Scala, Feb 2021; *Io Sono* (I Am) sold at Art-Rite auction house, Milan, May 2021 (went viral early Jun 2021).

### 1. The story
- Sardinian artist Salvatore Garau began making "immaterial sculptures": in Feb 2021 he marked a square of tape on the paving of Piazza della Scala in Milan and declared an invisible *Buddha in Contemplation* stood there.
- In May 2021 the Milan auction house Art-Rite sold his invisible sculpture *Io Sono* for EUR 15,000 (about US$18,300), above an estimate of EUR 6,000-9,000.
- The buyer received only a signed certificate of authenticity and instructions to display it in a private home in an empty space of about 150 x 150 cm, with no lighting required.
- Garau said it is not nothing but a vacuum, "nothing more than a space full of energy."
- In Jun 2021 the story went global; a Florida performer, Tom Miller, then threatened to sue, claiming he had shown an invisible sculpture (*Nothing*) first in 2016 in Gainesville.
- Garau kept going: another immaterial piece, *Davanti a te*, later sold in Milan for EUR 27,120 (Artnet, updated Oct 2025).

### 2. Why it went viral
Mechanic: **absurd price for literally nothing + emperor's-new-clothes joke**. The headline is the joke; nobody needs to see the work.
- Picked up by Artnet, Hypebeast, Complex, Nerdist, My Modern Met, Paris Match, Yahoo, Guardian Nigeria, Deseret, Washington Examiner, and Know Your Meme (which logged it as a meme news item).
- Endless "I have one in my garage" jokes; a scholar argued it only went viral *because* it had a price (Hans-Georg Moeller).
- Exact view counts unverified.

### 3. Open question to end on
If two people sell invisible sculptures, how do you prove one copied the other? And what would you see if you stood inside *Io Sono* right now?

### 4. Strangest true detail
The owner must keep a 150 x 150 cm area of their home empty forever - and an invisible sculpture triggered a real plagiarism threat over another invisible sculpture.

## The bored guard who drew eyes on a $1M avant-garde painting (Yekaterinburg)

**Date and place:** Damage found 7 Dec 2021; reported 20 Dec 2021; guard identified 8 Feb 2022 (went viral 9-11 Feb 2022); court sentence 29 Aug 2022. Yeltsin Center, Yekaterinburg, Russia.

### 1. The story
- The Yeltsin Center was showing "The World as Non-Objectivity", a Soviet avant-garde exhibition with works on loan from the State Tretyakov Gallery in Moscow.
- Among them was Anna Leporskaya's *Three Figures* (1932-34), a painting of three faceless figures, insured for 74.9 million rubles (about US$1M).
- On 7 Dec 2021 two visitors noticed that two of the faces now had eyes, drawn in ballpoint pen.
- The Center called the police on 20 Dec; police called the damage "insignificant" and declined to investigate, so the Culture Ministry complained to the Prosecutor General's Office.
- On 8 Feb 2022 the Center's director announced the culprit: a security guard (about 60) from a private contractor, who did it on his first day at work; the organiser said he was "bored," and he reportedly used a Yeltsin Center souvenir pen.
- The painting went back to the Tretyakov for restoration (insurer paid 250,000 rubles); the strokes were soft, so the damage was reversible.
- On 29 Aug 2022 a court sentenced the guard to 180 hours of compulsory labour and a psychiatric evaluation; his lawyer asked reporters: "So what now? Execute him? Hold a public flogging?"

### 2. Why it went viral
Mechanic: **absurd, relatable human mistake ("bored on day one") + a $1M price tag + a doodle anyone could draw**. It became an instant meme ("when you're bored at work").
- Within 48 hours: AP (via hundreds of US outlets), Newsweek, Business Insider, Artnet, Hypebeast, Hyperallergic, The Moscow Times, 1News NZ, Protothema, The Quint "social buzz".
- Second cycle in Aug 2022 with the sentence (ArtReview).
- Exact view counts unverified.

### 3. Open question to end on
Why did he give the faceless figures eyes - boredom, or did he think they needed to see? And did the painting look better with them?

### 4. Strangest true detail
He drew the eyes on his very first day on the job - guarding the paintings - with the museum's own branded pen.

## A 4-year-old breaks a 3,500-year-old jar - and the museum invites him back

**Date and place:** Late Aug 2024 (story broke 27-28 Aug 2024); return visit 30 Aug 2024; restored jar back on display Sep 2024. Hecht Museum, University of Haifa, Israel.

### 1. The story
- At the entrance of the Hecht Museum stood a Middle Bronze Age storage jar (about 2200-1500 BCE), probably used for wine or olive oil, one of very few found complete.
- It was shown without glass on purpose: the museum's founder believed there is "a special charm" in seeing archaeological finds without barriers.
- In late Aug 2024 a four-year-old visitor, curious about what was inside, pulled it slightly; it fell and smashed.
- His father said he was "in shock" and immediately told staff; the museum said the damage was not intentional, called it a "rare incident" and chose not to blame or punish the family.
- Instead it invited the family back: on 30 Aug 2024 the boy returned for a special visit and saw the restoration work under way; the museum said the jar would be "returned to its place in a short time."
- A conservator restored it and it went back on display in Sep 2024 (exact date to verify).

### 2. Why it went viral
Mechanic: **every parent's nightmare + a happy, wholesome twist (forgiveness)**. The story flips from destruction to kindness - shareable by everyone.
- Global coverage in 48 hours: AP, BBC, CBS, NBC, ABC, Fox News, Global News, LBC, Daily Beast, My Modern Met, NBC Montana and dozens of AP affiliates.
- Comments flooded with "my kid would do this" confessions (as summarised by the press).
- Exact view counts unverified.

### 3. Open question to end on
Should priceless objects be behind glass - or is the risk the price of really seeing history? And what was inside the jar that he so badly wanted to see?

### 4. Strangest true detail
The jar survived 3,500 years, wars and an excavation - and lasted until a four-year-old wanted to peek inside.
