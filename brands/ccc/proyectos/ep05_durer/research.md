# Research: Albrecht Dürer (Cool Culture Club, test episode 05)

| # | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Rhinoceros, woodcut, 1515. Dürer never saw the animal: he worked from an anonymous letter from Lisbon with a sketch by an unknown artist. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCrer%27s_Rhinoceros |
| 2 | He gave it a small twisted horn on its back; real rhinos have no such horn. | same |
| 3 | The real rhino drowned when its ship sank in a storm off the Ligurian coast (it was chained to the deck). | same |
| 4 | His rhino was taken as accurate until the late 18th century and appeared in German school textbooks as a faithful picture until the late 1930s. | same |
| 5 | He complained to the government of Venice about copies of his prints with his AD monogram; he won protection for the monogram, not for the compositions: an early case in intellectual-property law. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcantonio_Raimondi |
| 6 | Self-portrait, 1500 (Alte Pinakothek, Munich): frontal, signed with the AD monogram. Young Hare, watercolour, 1502 (Albertina). Praying Hands, 1508. Melencolia I, 1514. | Wikimedia Commons file pages (public domain) |
| 7 | Quote: "For in truth, art is embedded in nature; he who can extract it, has it." (Four Books on Human Proportion, 1528). | Dürer, Vier Bücher von menschlicher Proportion, 1528 |
