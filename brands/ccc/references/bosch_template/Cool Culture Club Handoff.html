Cool Culture Club: where the project stands

Everything decided and built so far for the Cool Culture Club shorts: the two presenter avatars, the Bosch episode assets, a reusable bricolage kit for artist portraits, the color system that ties them together, and the After Effects builds.

Project root: C:\Documentos\Trabajos Clientes\FL\Cool Culture Club\cool-culture-clubPrepared by Gian Orsi with Claude

Overview

Cool Culture Club is a creator account that celebrates artists and creators: classic painters like Hieronymus Bosch, and later music artists. Each episode is a vertical short (1080×1920, 30 fps) where a synthetic 3D presenter tells the story, intercut with the artist's work, a bricolage portrait of the artist and a quote card.

What exists today

  • Two presenters: a girl with a backwards orange-to-fuchsia cap on a green or coral background, and a guy with dreadlocks on a magenta-red background. Image and video prompts are below.
  • Bosch episode: script, presenter clips, a bricolage portrait (CCC_Bosch_Bricolage_Portrait_V1.mp4) and a quote card (CCC_Bosch_Quote_V1.mp4), each 6 s with sound.
  • Bricolage kit: 125 transparent PNG elements (gaffer tape, marker, paint, paper, stickers, hardware, print textures, frames) for building portraits of any artist.
  • Color system: the kit is graded to the presenters' palette, with a .cube LUT for After Effects.

The creative direction

Modern bricolage, not vintage collage: a black-and-white photocopy cutout of the artist, surrounded by fluorescent gaffer tape, Sharpie marks, vinyl stickers, acetate and office hardware. No sepia, aged paper, newspaper clippings or typewriter text. All color comes from the craft materials, and the subject stays black and white.

Presenter avatars

Both presenters are floating 3D art-toy heads with no body or neck. These are the final image prompts that produced the approved looks. Keep the identity lines unchanged when generating new poses.

What we learned writing them

  • Framing: spell out that the top of the cap, both ears, the chin and the ends of the hair sit inside the frame, and that the head fills about 60% of the frame height. Portrait aspect (4:5 or 2:3) helps long hair fit.
  • A backwards cap needs explaining: "strap and back panels facing forward over the forehead, brim pointing behind the head". Saying "backwards" alone gets ignored.
  • Gradients on fabric: describe a dyed ombré, with direction ("lower left to upper right"), so it doesn't look like a flat digital overlay.
  • Clean skin: list every unwanted mark (scars, stains, moles, freckles, redness, face paint, extra tattoos) and say the one tattoo is "the only mark anywhere on his skin".
  • Which side is "his left": models confuse the subject's left with the viewer's left. Say "on the right side of the image" if it lands on the wrong side.

Avatar video (Seedance 2.5)

Image-to-video in Seedance 2.5, with the avatar still as the start frame. Seedance 2.5 generates up to 30 s per clip.

How the clips are split

  • Each script has 5 avatar lines (A lines). Together they need about 29 s of speech, too tight for one 30 s clip with pauses, so they go into two clips: Clip A (L01 + L03 + L05, 20 s) and Clip B (L08 + L10, 16 s).
  • Between lines the avatar settles to a neutral face for under a second and blinks. Those pauses are the cut points where the artwork shots (B lines) go in.
  • One clip per line remains a fallback when a long clip drifts in identity or lip sync.

Performance rules that worked

  • More expressive than a real person: big eyebrow moves, cheeks squashing, wide mouth shapes.
  • Head turns only when the line calls for one (a side glance, a doubtful aside, a dreamy drift), then back to the lens for every punchline.
  • Natural blinks every 2 to 4 s, quick blinks on emphasis.
  • Timestamped beats such as [2.5-4s] tie each expression to a word.
  • The negative prompt goes at the end as an Avoid: line. If hands or a body appear, move it to the top.
  • Hand gestures from the original script actions are rewritten as face or head moves, because the presenters have no hands.

Voice workflow (from the earlier episodes)

The voice Seedance generates gives the exact lip sync. ElevenLabs Speech-to-Speech then swaps its timbre to the series voice, keeping rhythm and pauses. The B lines use direct ElevenLabs voiceover, so the voice matches across the whole video.

For any name the model may mispronounce, put a guide in brackets next to it, for example "Hieronymus Bosch [heer-ON-ih-mus Bosh]".

How it was made

  1. One sheet per group, generated with GPT Image 2.6 on Weave, background: transparent, high quality, 1536×1024 PNG, about 11 credits per sheet. Every prompt ends with: "Isolated elements on a transparent background, flat top-down view, spread out with clear space between them, none touching, none cut off by the edge, no shadow, no background."
  2. split_sheets.py cuts each sheet into one trimmed PNG per element, using alpha connected components. The merge distance depends on the sheet: small for solid shapes that sit close together (stickers 3, paper 4), large for strokes and dot patterns that must stay one piece (marker 14, paint 18, print 14).
  3. ccc_grade.py grades every layer to the presenter palette into layers_ccc/.

Making a new artist's portrait

  1. Turn a public-domain or cleared portrait of the artist into the cutout with the prompt below (GPT Image 2.6 edit, transparent background).
  2. Make a name label: black gaffer tape with the name in white paint marker. Spell the name out letter by letter in the prompt.
  3. Grade both with python ccc_grade.py <src> <dst>.
  4. Edit the LAYOUT list in compose.py and run it to get the PNG and the layout JSON, then build the comp in AE (next section).
Subject cutout prompt (any artist)
Turn the person in this image into a modern photocopy cutout: same face, same features, same clothing, head and shoulders only. Crisp high-contrast black and white like a fresh laser photocopy with a fine halftone dot texture, deep blacks and clean whites. Cut out by hand along the silhouette with a thin even white paper border. Isolated on a transparent background, no shadow.

What didn't work

  • Bosch creature stickers (owl, fish with legs, strawberry): rejected because they only work for Bosch. The kit has to be generic.
  • Hell-panel crop from the painting: blocked by OpenAI's safety filter because of the nudity, and charged anyway. Crops of real paintings, if ever needed, are better made locally from the file.
  • Old-paper look: the brief is modern. Keep "aged paper, sepia, newspaper clippings, typewriter text" in the avoid lists.

Color correcting in After Effects

  • Default: build from layers_ccc.
  • Or use the neon originals with an adjustment layer: Apply Color LUT loaded with CCC_presenter_palette.cube.
  • To shift one color family everywhere: Hue/Saturation on the adjustment layer, with one channel selected.
  • To recolor one element: Change to Color or Tint on that layer only.

  • Each line of the quote sits on its own torn cream strip (K_torn_paper_06), sized to the text: strip width = text width + 120 px, height = text height + 70 px.
  • Strips wipe on left to right at 0.60, 0.95, 1.30 and 1.65 s. Each line fades up word by word (Words Fade Up) 0.15 s after its strip.
  • Attribution in Bahnschrift Light on black tape at 2.15 to 2.30 s, also Words Fade Up. Tracking Settle was tried and spread the text past the frame.

The hold: stop-motion jitter

Once a piece lands, its rotation gets a small jitter at 6 fps, so the frame looks handmade instead of frozen. A strip and its line of text share one seed so the words never slide on the paper.

Rotation expression
// stop-motion jitter once the piece has landed
if (time < inPoint + 0.9) value; else { posterizeTime(6); seedRandom(index, true); value + (wiggle(1, 1.2) - value); }

Amplitude: about 0.3° for the subject, 0.4–0.6° for paper and tape, 1–2° for stickers and marks. For a strip and its text, replace index with the same number on both layers.

Sound

add_sound makes the sound effects in code (pops, swishes, a hit, ticks), so they're ours to ship. Put the cues on exact landing times: for example, the portrait's hit is at 1.06 s, the cutout's first impact.

Checks

Run check_layout before rendering. It measures text against what's behind the layer, so text sitting on an image card (the cream strips) shows as low contrast when it isn't. Those warnings can stay. Fix the real ones: the magenta quote mark was 1.2:1 on the gradient and became cream.

Problems and fixes

The Motion DNA bridge stops answering

Symptom: ae_status times out while AE is open and responding, and jobs pile up in bridge/inbox. The bridge's polling chain has stopped. Re-arm it from a terminal:

AfterFX.exe -s "if ($.global.SS_BRIDGE_ON && $.global.SS_BRIDGE_TICK) { try { app.cancelTask($.global.SS_BRIDGE_TASK); } catch (e) {} $.global.SS_BRIDGE_TASK = app.scheduleTask($.global.SS_BRIDGE_TICK, 500, false); }"

Don't run ss_bridge.jsx again: running it twice toggles the bridge off.

Wipe Reveal reveals right to left

The preset sets the Linear Wipe angle to 90°, which reveals the right side first. Set SS Wipe › Transition Angle to 270° for left to right (text, tape) and 0° for top to bottom (drips). The direction option in apply_preset doesn't change it. Worth fixing in Motion DNA itself.

add_sound fails with "Permission denied"

AE holds the old WAV open. Remove the Motion DNA sound layer and its footage item, run app.purge(PurgeTarget.ALL_CACHES), then run add_sound again.

Weave flow has no inputs

The CCC Avatar Test flow (app.weavy.ai/flow/NT708RqEcXqrk5zcQAQrSX) returns no inputs or outputs over MCP. Publish it as a tool with the image node, the prompt node and the output exposed before it can be driven from Claude. Meanwhile, models run directly with weave_run_model.

Merged or broken elements after splitting

Two stickers sitting close together merged into one, and a sparkle burst broke into 8 dashes. Tune the merge distance per sheet in split_sheets.py, and group small pieces that belong together.

Renders land in the Motion DNA repo

render_comp writes to renders/mcp/ inside the Motion DNA repo. Move each file to 06_Renders right away. Project files never stay in the repo.

Music on Shorts

Cool Culture Club is a creator account, not a brand, which gives it access to most of the in-app music libraries.

  • Account types: Instagram Creator (not Business), TikTok Personal (not Business), and any YouTube channel. Business accounts lose most popular songs.
  • Add music inside each app, not in the export. Edit to the chosen section of the song with a muted guide track, export with voiceover and sound effects only, then add the song in the app at the same start point.
  • In-app music doesn't cover: boosting or paid promotion, sponsored posts, re-uploading the file elsewhere, or songs missing from the library. On YouTube, Shorts with music share revenue with the rights holders.
  • Visuals of music artists: album covers and photos are owned by labels and photographers, and in-app music licenses don't cover them. Use press kit images with credit, or ask the artist (and offer a Collab post). Don't make AI likenesses of real musicians.
  • Working directly with a band: get written permission from whoever owns the master and the publishing, saying whether it covers organic posts only or ads too. Have the distributor allowlist the accounts in Content ID and Meta Rights Manager. Ask for WAV masters and stems.

Platform rules change often and vary by country. For anything paid, check with a lawyer or the client's legal team.
